Four shapes of a loss surface, and what the Hessian sees

The Hessian H is the matrix of second derivatives of the loss: how the surface bends in each direction at a point. Its eigenvalues are the bends along its main directions: positive bends up, zero is flat, negative bends down. The influence function needs H⁻¹, so it needs every bend to be nonzero.
