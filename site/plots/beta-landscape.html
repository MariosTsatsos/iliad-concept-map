One example's weight β and the loss surface

Two weights, w1 and w2. The loss is L(w; β) = (all other data) + β × (loss on example i). The other data make a bowl centred at (−1, −0.5). Example i wants w1 + w2 = 2. β is the weight of example i: β = 1 is normal training, β = 0 removes the example. Move β and watch the surface tilt and bend, and the minimum move. What to look for

At β = 1 the red and blue points coincide. The influence function predicts the minimum by a straight line from β = 1 (a first-order step, −H⁻¹ × gradient of example i's loss). The grey curve is the true path of the minimum; the further β is from 1, the more the line and the curve part.

The cloud is the posterior, density ∝ exp(−L). The Bayesian influence function asks how its centre moves with β. With H given a zero and β = 0 there is no single minimum: the floor is a line, H cannot be inverted, and the cloud spreads along it (cut off by the edge of the plot).

Caveat: true as long as observable = loss.
